Corporate & Institutional Banking · Client Lifecycle Management

The client is not a form. The client is a graph.

Onboarding is the process of discovering, evidencing and approving a client knowledge graph: the legal entity, who owns and controls it, where it is taxed, how regulators classify it, and how risky it is. This design makes that graph the explicit product of the process, projected with strict lineage from a set of granular, purpose-specific domain data objects, and produced by a deterministic BPMN backbone that delegates work to agents wherever policy allows.

1. Executive summary

Corporate client onboarding today is run as a sequence of forms and checklists, with the client's real shape — who owns it, who controls it, where it is taxed, how it is regulated — reconstructed by people from documents at every stage and re-keyed into systems that do not agree with each other. This design turns that around: the output of onboarding is a client knowledge graph, produced deliberately, evidenced at every node, and reusable across the bank.

The approach rests on five moves. Policy is expressed as versioned rule packs rather than code. Client data is held in granular, purpose-specific domain data objects (JSON), each with one owner and one reason to change. The knowledge graph is a strict projection of those objects, so every node carries lineage back to a field, a source and a decision. A conventional BPMN process provides the deterministic backbone that regulators and control functions expect. Inside that process, work is delegated to agents: deterministic agents run rules and call systems; probabilistic agents read documents, draft and summarise, and may only propose.

The result is an onboarding platform that is schema-flexible where data lives, contract-governed where data moves, and explainable end to end — a UBO, a FATCA status or a risk rating can each be traced to the document page, the rule and the reviewer that produced it.

What the bank gets. One evidenced view of the client that the RM, KYC, tax, regulatory operations and financial crime all read from. Related parties mastered once and shared — a director or beneficial owner is screened and verified once, not per client. Periodic review as a replay of the same process against the same graph under the current policy version, rather than a fresh onboarding. A network view for financial crime: when a party hits a list, every connected client is one traversal away. And a control story that separates what agents decide from what agents suggest.

What this document asks for. Validation of the conceptual design — the graph model, the domain data objects, the lineage contract, the orchestration and the agent boundaries — before detailed design starts. 2. Context and approach

The problem being solved

Onboarding a corporate or institutional client is a policy-driven investigation. The bank must establish the legal entity, unwrap its ownership to the ultimate beneficial owners, determine its tax status under FATCA and CRS, classify it under every regulatory regime its products trigger, screen it and its related parties, and rate its risk — and it must be able to show its work to an auditor or a regulator years later.

Across 50+ markets the same investigation is run with different forms, different systems and different interpretations of a global policy plus local deltas. Client data is captured as flat attributes on a case, related parties are duplicated case by case, and evidence is attached to a file rather than to the fact it supports. The cost shows up as long cycle times, repeated client outreach, low confidence in cross-sell, and a periodic review process that effectively re-onboards.

The approach in five moves

Move 1Policy as dataGlobal policy and country deltas become versioned rule packs in a Digital Policy Engine: thresholds, decision trees, document requirements, classification tests. Every case pins the version it was decided under.
Move 2Client as domain data objectsThe client is described by granular JSON objects — definition, identifiers, addresses, ownership, tax, regulatory, screening, risk — each owned by one stage, each attribute carrying its source. Related parties are their own aggregate.
Move 3Graph as projectionChange events on the objects are projected by declarative rules into a property graph. Nothing is authored in the graph; every node and edge is stamped with the object, path and rule that produced it.
Move 4BPMN as backboneA conventional process model owns stages, gates, parallelism, timers and human queues. It is the deterministic, auditable spine that policy and control functions can read.
Move 5Agents on the leavesTasks are delegated to narrow-skill agents. Deterministic agents run rules and call systems and may commit. Probabilistic agents extract, draft, summarise and flag, and may only propose; a validator or a human accepts.

4. Design tenets and boundaries

Six commitments shape everything below. If any of them is wrong for the bank, the rest of the design changes, so they are the first thing to validate.

Schema-flexible storage, contract-governed data

"Schema-less" is honoured at the storage tier: the graph store imposes no fixed shape, and domain data objects are stored as JSON documents. It is not honoured at the boundary: every domain data object carries a versioned contract in a schema registry, with an explicit extension namespace so a new regulatory attribute can be added by a rule pack tomorrow without a release. Flexibility is where data lives; discipline is at every point where data moves.

The graph is a projection, never an author

No process, agent or user writes to the knowledge graph directly. Domain data objects are the system of record; the graph is rebuilt from them through change events. This is what makes strict lineage possible: a node can only exist if a field exists, and the projection rule that created it is recorded on the node.

Related parties are independent aggregates

A director, beneficial owner or controlling person is mastered once, in a Related Party domain with its own identifiers, verification and screening. A client's graph holds only the association: who, in what role, with what interest, evidenced how. Priya Menon in the example is a UBO of Meridian Agritrade and a director of Menon Textiles; she is one node, screened once, and both client graphs point to her.

Every fact has a source, or it is not a fact

Each attribute carries provenance: which system, document, vendor feed or person asserted it, which agent or task recorded it, when, under which policy version, and with what confidence. This is what turns a data model into a defensible KYC file, and it is what stops a probabilistic agent from inventing an ownership percentage.

Deterministic backbone, agents on the leaves

The process is a BPMN model: stages, gates, human tasks, timers, escalations. Inside it, tasks are delegated to agents. Deterministic agents run rules and call APIs and can commit data. Probabilistic agents extract, summarise, draft and detect anomalies, and can only propose. A proposal becomes a fact after a deterministic validator or a human accepts it.

Policy is data, not code

Unwrapping thresholds, risk factor weights, document requirements, FATCA and CRS decision trees and regulatory classification tests are expressed as versioned rule packs (global policy plus country deltas). The case records the policy version it was assessed under, so a periodic review can replay the decision and diff it against the current rules.

Four boundaries that must hold

How a fact travels through the layers

Take one attribute — Lars Eriksen's 15% shareholding in Meridian Holdings B.V. A probabilistic extraction agent reads the shareholder register uploaded by the client and proposes {party: "Lars Eriksen", pct: 15, page: 3} with 0.94 confidence. The deterministic Ownership Unwrapping Agent validates that the proposal reconciles with the KvK registry extract and that percentages at that level sum to 100, then commits it to Entity.OwnershipStructure with provenance pointing at both the document and the registry. The change event reaches the graph projector, which creates the edge Lars Eriksen —OWNS 15%→ Meridian Holdings B.V. stamped with the DDO id and JSON path. The unwrapping agent then computes his 15% indirect interest in the client, above the 10% threshold for a risk rating C, which creates the UBO_OF edge and, because he is a PEP, fires the EDD trigger in the risk rule pack. No one typed anything into the graph.

Semantics the graph must preserve

Observed, accepted, derivedAn extraction or a vendor record is an observation. An accepted fact has passed validation and authority checks. A derived fact (a UBO, a risk rating) cites its accepted inputs and the deterministic logic that produced it. Rejected and disputed assertions are kept for audit but never rendered as truth; the graph shows accepted and derived facts only.
Two clocksValid time is when a fact was true in the world (a holding from 14 May 2018); recorded time is when the bank knew it. Both are on every DDO version and every projected edge, so the bank can answer "who owned the client on 1 June, according to what we knew on 10 June?" — the question a regulator actually asks.
Relationship facts are first-classAn ownership edge is a projection of an ownership record with its own id, evidence and validity. Where several parties or rights make up one relationship (a joint control agreement, a trust with settlor, trustee, protector and beneficiaries), the graph uses a relationship node with participant links. Every link still has an owning object and path.
Reference nodes are governed too. Jurisdiction, industry, product, booking entity and policy version are shared nodes. They are projected from versioned reference-data objects with the same lineage stamp; display-only labels and UI groupings are never business nodes, and no unowned "helper" nodes are allowed in the graph.
Why ownership and legal hierarchy are separate edges. A parent in the consolidation hierarchy is usually also the majority owner, but not always: a fund is the 30% owner of Meridian Holdings B.V. without being its parent, and a trust can be the parent for consolidation without a person "owning" it. Keeping PARENT_OF and OWNS distinct lets the UBO computation ignore consolidation and lets group exposure reporting ignore minority holders.

7. Domain data object catalogue

Each domain data object (DDO) has one purpose, one accountable owner, and one projection into the graph. Three families exist: Entity.* objects belong to a client; RelatedParty.* objects belong to a party and are referenced, never copied, by clients; Case.* objects belong to a process instance and hold obligations, proposals, decisions and snapshots — the control record around the client facts. The unit of storage is a versioned object; the unit of consistency is an aggregate or an explicitly coordinated command. The complete client is never one mutable document.

Granularity test used for the catalogue. An object stays separate when (a) it has a different owning stage or reviewer, (b) it has a different change cadence at periodic review, or (c) it has a different data residency or access classification. Tax and regulatory classification are separated for reason (a); screening and risk for reason (b); related party verification for reason (c) — identity documents carry stricter handling than a company's industry code. Control relationships are split from ownership because a control right has its own evidence, validity and reviewer, and because a person with 8% and a board-appointment right must be assessable independently of any percentage. When a collection inside an object grows large or gains its own approval lifecycle (a screening run with hundreds of hits, an EDD investigation), it becomes its own object with a stable id; array positions are only safe as references inside an immutable version.

Domain data objects for this client

Every object shares one envelope: a $contract (which DDO and version), a $key, an $audit block pinned to a task and policy version and carrying both clocks (validFrom for when the fact holds in the world, updatedAt for when the bank recorded it), an ext namespace for registered attributes added by rule packs after the contract was published, and a $provenance ledger that records, per JSON path, where the value came from and who accepted it. Conventions worth noting: identifiers of things that can be referenced (holdings, roles, findings, documents) are stable ids, not array positions; percentages and amounts that feed calculations are stored as exact decimals; an unknown value is recorded with a status and a reason, because null must never be read as "not applicable" or "no risk". 10. Ownership and control

Ownership unwrapping is the crux of the graph, and the place where a percentage is most often mistaken for a conclusion. The method separates discovery, calculation, control assessment and determination, applies each under the jurisdiction's own rules, and refuses to publish a UBO result it cannot defend.

12. BPMN orchestration

One versioned BPMN process, five stages, five gates. The engine owns sequencing, parallelism, timers, escalation and the human task queues; agents own the work inside tasks. No LLM ever selects the process path or decides whether a mandatory control can be skipped. Lanes below show who performs each task. Gates are human decisions by design — an approver closes a stage or reopens any task in it, and reopened tasks re-run through the same model rather than through an ad hoc path.

Stage gates G1–G5. Tax and Enrich run in parallel after G1 because tax classification depends on ownership only when CRS controlling persons are required; the model joins them before KYC. EDD is a conditional sub-process inside KYC. Any gate can send the case back to a named task in an earlier stage.

Orchestration rules that apply to every stage

RuleWhat it means in the BPMN model
Task contractEvery task declares the DDOs it reads and the DDOs it may write. The task router enforces this; an agent cannot write outside its contract.
Policy pinThe case pins a policy version at G0. A mid-case policy change raises a signal event; the impact-analysis service decides whether to re-run affected tasks or continue on the pinned version, and the decision is recorded in Case.Context.
Proposal handlingProbabilistic agent tasks always output proposals. The model routes each proposal to a deterministic validator task; validator failures route to a human verification task. Only validator or human tasks commit.
Reopen semanticsA gate reviewer can reopen any task in the stage. The engine cancels downstream tokens in that stage, re-runs the task and everything that depends on it, and returns the case to the gate. Reopen count and reason are recorded for BAM.

Timers, failures and side effects

Timers and failuresBoundary timers handle overdue outreach and provider outages. Only transient technical failures are retried, with limits and backoff. A business rejection produces a reasoned result, never a retry loop. Dead-letter incidents are owned by operations, with the case visibly blocked rather than silently stalled.
Safe external side effectsEvery provisioning call carries a case/task/command idempotency key and a correlation id persisted before the call. After a timeout the worker queries external status before retrying. Compensation for an opened account is an authorised restriction or closure — never deletion of audit history.
In-flight changeProcess and policy releases are pinned per case. An urgent policy change raises an impact decision and a controlled migration or re-evaluation; rules are never swapped under a running task. A readiness snapshot taken under the old version becomes stale and cannot authorise activation.

13. Agent design and execution authority

Two execution modes with different rights, enforced by the runtime rather than by convention. Determinism is a property of the execution boundary: a worker that invokes explicit rules and APIs in a fixed sequence is deterministic; the moment an LLM selects the next action or interprets a business rule at run time, that part is probabilistic — even at temperature zero. Deterministic agents may commit to contracted objects; probabilistic agents may only propose.

Deterministic agents

Fixed tool sequence, bound knowledge (a rule pack, an API, a document index), no free-text reasoning on the outcome path. Where an LLM is used at all it is for parsing an input into a structured call, and the structured call is what is logged and replayed. They exist as agents rather than plain services because they compose several tools, handle partial data, and decide when to stop — the ownership unwrapper is the canonical case.

Probabilistic agents

LLM-driven, ReAct or structured-output. Used where the input is unstructured (documents, web pages, free-text client responses) or the output is prose (outreach, narratives, summaries). They never see a decision they could bias: the adverse media agent summarises hits, it does not rate the client; the extraction agent proposes a percentage, it does not compute a UBO.

15. Lifecycle and reuse

A change to a shared party can affect many clients, and the bank must propagate impact without propagating conclusions. Facts are shared; assessments are scoped; invalidation is explicit.

Priya Menon's identity is corrected

A new RelatedParty.Profile version is published with evidence; the earlier value is retained. Dependency indexes find every relationship and assessment that read the old version — Meridian and Menon Textiles both. Re-screening runs where rules require it. Each client keeps its own case and its own risk outcome.

An owner sells a holding

The previous holding's valid interval is closed and the new holding published. Ownership paths are recalculated at the effective date. Past assessments that relied on the superseded fact are flagged for review without rewriting the historical decision record.

A PEP hit is cleared

The adjudication is stored on RelatedParty.Screening with identity evidence, reviewer and provider hit reference. Another client inherits the disposition only if identity, hit, scope, freshness and list-version rules permit. PEP status and sanctions status stay distinct concepts.

Two party records are merged

RelatedParty.Resolution records the decision; old ids become aliases and redirects; decision provenance is preserved; affected current relationships are re-projected. A wrong match can be split with a controlled undo — history is never rewritten to pretend the duplicate did not exist.

Storage and projection

DDOs in PostgreSQL JSONB (one table per aggregate family, versioned rows, JSON Schema validation on write) is the pragmatic default given the existing stack; a document database is equivalent. The provenance ledger is append-only in the same store. CDC publishes change events to the Solace event mesh; the graph projector is one consumer among several (client 360, reporting, downstream systems). The graph is Neo4j or Neptune and holds nothing that cannot be rebuilt from DDOs; recovery restores canonical versions, evidence references, audit records and event positions consistently, and activation stays gated until projection consistency is proven.

Contract evolution

Core fields change through versioned contracts with backward compatibility; rule-pack-driven attributes land in ext with a registered dotted key and are promoted to core when they stabilise. Projection rules are stored with the schema registry and versioned together with the contracts they read.

Orchestration and agents

Camunda 8 for a BPMN-native process with human tasks, DMN for genuinely tabular rules (FATCA, regulatory applicability), Zeebe workers for agent invocation. Temporal remains the better fit if the bank later prefers code-first workflows; the task contract and proposal pattern do not depend on the engine. Agents are LangGraph packages with a manifest (mode, scope, knowledge, skills, MCP tools) matching the Onboard.AI agent framework; cib-policy-rules-mcp and cib-idp-mcp are the shared MCP servers. Idempotency keys and run traces are stored with the case.

Scale and residency

Recovery objectives, regional topology and traversal limits depend on the bank's volumes, residency map and availability targets, which are inputs to detailed design (section 17). The conceptual separation of responsibilities does not change with those numbers.

17. Decisions to validate

These are the choices in this document that most deserve challenge before detailed design starts.

1. Related Party as a bank-wide master. The design assumes the Related Party aggregate is shared across all clients and all onboarding jurisdictions. That gives the network view financial crime wants, but it makes the party record subject to every jurisdiction's residency rules at once. Alternative: regional party masters with a global resolution index.
2. Graph as a pure projection. Nothing is authored in the graph. This is the cleanest lineage story but it means graph-native analytics (community detection, risk propagation) produce derived DDOs rather than annotating the graph. Confirm the bank is comfortable that analytics outputs go through the same DDO contract.
3. Ownership and legal hierarchy as separate objects. Some vendors deliver them as one tree. Separating them costs a reconciliation step at Intake; merging them risks UBO computation counting consolidation as ownership. The design keeps them separate.
4. Tax in parallel with Enrich. Parallelism saves days but CRS controlling-person determination for a passive NFE needs the completed ownership structure. The model joins on that condition; confirm the tax team accepts a conditional dependency rather than a fixed sequence.
5. Deterministic agents with commit rights. The line between "deterministic agent" and "service" is that the agent composes tools and decides when to stop. If the bank's control function prefers, these can be re-labelled as orchestrated services with identical behaviour; the label matters for model-risk classification.
6. Screening reuse across clients. The 12-month reuse rule for party screening and 24-month for ID&V are placeholders. Policy owns these numbers; the design only requires that they are rule-pack values, not code.
7. Extension namespace governance. ext lets a rule pack add attributes without a release. Without a registration step it becomes a junk drawer. The proposal is that a rule pack cannot deploy an ext key that is not described in the schema registry.
8. Scope model. Every assessment and entitlement keyed by bank entity × jurisdiction × product × time. This multiplies assessment records; the alternative — one category per client per regime — is simpler and wrong for multi-entity clients. Confirm the operating model can work with scoped outcomes.
9. Approval authority and straight-through lanes. Where policy permits, a deterministic rule may close a gate without a person (low-risk, fully externally evidenced clients). The bank must define exactly which lanes are straight-through and which exceptions require a human, and the delegated authority each approver holds.
10. Bitemporal depth. Valid time and recorded time on every object version costs storage and query complexity. The design treats it as mandatory for ownership, control, roles and assessments; confirm whether it is required for descriptive objects (addresses, business profile) or whether recorded time alone suffices there.

19. Glossary

Domain data object (DDO)
A versioned JSON document describing one aspect of a client (or a related party), with one owning stage and a provenance ledger. The system of record.
Knowledge graph
A property graph of clients, parties, jurisdictions, classifications, outcomes and evidence, projected from DDOs by declarative rules.
Lineage
The stamp on every node and edge naming the DDO, JSON path, projection rule, source, agent, task, policy version and validator behind it.
Related party
An individual or non-client entity with a role in one or more clients. Mastered once as an independent aggregate; clients hold only the association.
UBO
Ultimate beneficial owner: a natural person who owns, directly or indirectly, at or above the policy threshold, or who controls the client where ownership dilutes.
Rule pack
A versioned, machine-readable set of policy rules (global plus country deltas) served by the Digital Policy Engine and pinned to a case.
Deterministic agent
An agent with a fixed tool sequence and no free-form reasoning in its outcome path. Composes rules and system calls; may commit to contracted DDOs.
Probabilistic agent
An LLM-driven agent used for extraction, synthesis, drafting and anomaly detection. Produces proposals with citations and confidence; cannot commit.
Proposal
A candidate value for a DDO path, with citation and confidence, awaiting a deterministic validator or a human before it becomes a fact.
Gate
A human decision that closes a stage after reviewing the subgraph it produced, with the ability to reopen any task in that stage.
Readiness snapshot
The immutable set of object versions, requirement states and consistency watermarks a gate decision was taken on; referenced by the activation token.
Requirement ledger
The case's list of atomic obligations derived from the rule packs, each with origin, acceptance criteria, owner and blocking status.
Scope
Bank entity × jurisdiction × product or service × validity period — the key of every assessment and entitlement.
